You can search for Pokémon that fit specific criteria by typing in various terms, or "tags," that may be associated with the Pokémon you're looking for. There are several broad tag categories that apply to many Pokémon (such as "red" for any red Pokémon token, or "water" for any Water-Type Pokémon), and these categories will have a corresponding button in one of the sections below. There are some tags that apply to only a few or even a single Pokémon. Any tag can be typed into the search bar, even if there's already a button for it. You can find examples of the tags you can use below.

Broadly speaking, the tags you use will apply with AND, OR, or NOT logic. When you press a filter button below and it turns blue, it will use OR logic. Some sections (Evolution State, Pokémon Types, Token Abilities, and Unique Properties) have their own "Match" button, which starts on "Match: Any" (OR logic). Pressing it switches to "Match: All" (AND logic) and turns that section's selected buttons green so you can tell at a glance which mode you're in.

If you press and hold a filter button, it will turn red and apply NOT logic, meaning that tag is excluded from the search. You can click the button again to reset it.

Typing directly into the search box has its own shorthand for AND, OR, and NOT, so you don't need a button for a tag to combine it this way. Separating tags with a space combines them the same way clicking their buttons would: tags from different sections always AND together ("water gen1" finds Water-Types from Generation 1, since Types and Generation are different sections), while tags that share a section combine however that section is currently set — OR by default, or AND if you've flipped that section to "Match: All" (While the Evolution State Match button is set to Any, "splitevo camega" finds anything that is either part of a branched Evolution Chain or capable of Mega Evolution; while it's set to All, only those that fulfill both those requirements simultaneously are found). Join tags with a slash to OR them regardless of section ("water/ground" finds anything that's Water-type, Ground-type, or both). Join tags with a plus to AND them within that one term regardless of section ("water+ground" finds only Pokémon that are both Water and Ground-type). Start a term with a dash to exclude it, and this combines with slash or plus too — "-water/ground" excludes anything Water-type or Ground-type, while "-water+ground" excludes only Pokémon that are both at once.

Tags that apply to broad categories of Pokémon such that they have a filter button assigned are as follows:

  • Generation: Which generation of Pokémon a given token belongs to.
  • Regional Pokédex: These tags are organized such that they include all Pokémon from a given video game's Pokédex.
  • Evolution State: These tags correspond to a Pokémon's status as it relates to Evolution. Under "Match: All", combinations that no Pokémon could ever have (like two different Stage tags at once) are blocked automatically.
  • Token Color: The color of the Pokémon's game token.
  • Pokémon Types: The Pokémon's Type(s). Under "Match: All", selection is capped at two, since no Pokémon has more than two types.
  • Token Abilities: Any Abilities the Pokémon may have that can be used outside of Battle.
  • Unique Properties: A few gameplay-relevant traits a Pokémon may have that don't cleanly fit into other categories, and more than one may apply to a Pokémon.

More unique tags that may apply to a small number of Pokémon follow the formats detailed below.

  • National Pokédex Number: Formatted as "000" for Pokémon whose NDex number is below 1000, and "0000" for Pokémon whose NDex number is 1000 or higher. Pikachu is 025, and Pecharunt is 1025. This tag will always apply to a single Pokémon (including any alternate forms that Pokémon has).
  • Pokémon Name: The name of the Pokémon. Other Pokémon that are related by evolution will appear as well. For instance, by typing "jigglypuff" you will find Jigglypuff, Wigglytuff, and Igglybuff. (If a Pokémon has a space or punctuation in its name like Mr. Mime, I've done my best to add different permutations of the name to make it easier to search for. For instance, "mrmime," "mr.mime," "mr mime," "mr. mime," "mr," "mr.," and "mime," should all find Mr. Mime and members of its Evolution Chain.)
